The Joy of Jewish Music and Dance · 2026–27 Season

Introducing our new lineup of events featuring talented purveyors of a variety of musical styles within and beyond the pale. Our fourth season of monthly offerings continue to bring together students and workshop leaders to sing songs, play music and folkdance together.

SUNDAY, NOVEMBER 15, 2026  |  2–5 PM

The Romanian/Klezmer Connection

Romanian melodies meet klezmer—with a surprise visiting vocal star and an afternoon of hands-on music and dance.

With Peter Bonos, Kim Goldov, Aaron Kierbel, and Čardak dance leaders Janetta Redžić and Alexandru Sandrovschii.

Join Us November 15 →

Meet Your November Teachers

Peter Bonos

Trumpet · Orchestra Euphonos

Bay Area musician and bandleader specializing in klezmer, romanian and Balkan styles. Peter leads Orchestra Euphonos and produces the Berkeley Balkan Bacchanal.

Kim Goldov

Violin · Visiting from Seattle

Visiting from Seattle, a longtime klezmer and Eastern European music performer, Kim plays with the Freylakh Klezmer Band and Talne Trio, bringing decades of ensemble experience to the workshop.

Aaron Kierbel

Percussion · RhythmALLogy

Aaron has played klezmer for over 20 years with bands such as the Gonifs and Kugelplex. An experienced educator, Aaron’s participatory workshops through rhythm, movement, and play.

Janetta Redžić

Dance · Čardak

Founder of the pan-Balkan ensemble Čardak, Janetta teaches through connection and shared movement. She is joined by Alexandru Sandrovschii, a Moldovan-Jewish member of Čardak, to lead the dance workshop.

SUNDAY, DECEMBER 13, 2026  |  2–5 PM

Veretski Pass & Bruce Bierman

Learn klezmer with Veretski Pass. Explore Jewish dance with Bruce Bierman. Then bring the music and movement together in a shared dance party.

Join Us December 13 →

Learn. Play. Dance.

Choose music or dance instruction, then gather to share what you’ve learned and enjoy a dance party. All instruments, singers, and dancers are welcome. Basic instrumental proficiency is recommended for musicians.
